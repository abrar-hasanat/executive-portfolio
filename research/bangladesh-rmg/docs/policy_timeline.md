# Historical policy exposure and timeline

Accessed 26 September 2026. These records establish historical facts, not current tariff advice.

## Exposure audit

The [July 2013 USTR GSP guide](https://www.ustr.gov/sites/default/files/GSP%20Guidebook%20July%202013.pdf), section on eligible articles, identifies most textiles and apparel among statutory exclusions. GSP eligibility is indicated by A, A+ or A* in the HTS special-rate column; country eligibility, origin rules and actual claims also matter. A low or zero rate under another agreement is not GSP access for Bangladesh.

Two illustrative eight-digit tariff checks in the **2013 Basic HTS**, not the present schedule:

| HTS line | Description | Column 1 general rate | GSP symbol in special column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6109.10.00 | Cotton T-shirts and related knitted/crocheted garments | 16.5% | No A, A+ or A* |
| 6203.42.40 | Other men's/boys' cotton trousers and related garments | 16.6% | No A, A+ or A* |

Sources: [chapter 61](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/docs/tata/hts/bychapter/1300c61.pdf) and [chapter 62](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/docs/tata/hts/bychapter/1300c62.pdf), exact line entries. These are illustrative checks, not an exhaustive tariff-line census. They establish that representative apparel rates were already positive. No weighted tariff exposure estimate is constructed from two-digit chapter data.

[USTR's 27 June 2013 announcement](https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2013/june/michael-froman-gsp-bangladesh), Background, reports $34.7 million actually entered under GSP from Bangladesh in 2012. Leading categories were tobacco, sports equipment, porcelain china and plastic products. This is aggregate actual preference use, not total eligible trade and not a product-level utilization panel. Such a panel was not acquired. A causal study of eligible products would need historical HTS8/10 eligibility and claims, treatment of unused preferences, and country-origin compliance. The present project makes no claim to have completed that larger audit.

Date discrepancy: the [CBP correction](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/82fe9c) explicitly gives 3 September 2013; the [2014 HTS change record](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/docs/tata/hts/bychapter/1400chgs.pdf) lists 31 August 2013. The discrepancy remains unresolved here. The annual descriptive analysis does not depend on choosing between these dates. A daily or monthly customs study must resolve entry-level treatment before estimating an effect.

The [2015 renewal notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2015-07-28/pdf/2015-18459.pdf) covers eligible entries from 1 August 2013 through 28 July 2015 but explicitly excludes Bangladesh from retroactive treatment. A common lapse and later differentiated refunds mean that a simple post-2013 country dummy cannot adequately represent eligible-product tariff exposure.

## US and EU arrangements

The [European Parliament's 23 May 2013 resolution](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/TA-7-2013-0230_EN.pdf), paragraphs 7-8, confirms Bangladesh's duty- and quota-free access under Everything But Arms and discusses conditional withdrawal. This historical record supports the contrast with US apparel exclusions. It does not establish an actual EU withdrawal in 2013. EU sourcing is not analyzed and is not treated as an unaffected control. No claim about 2026 eligibility or current legal obligations is made.

## Buyer governance

The [original Accord](https://internationalaccord.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2013-Accord.pdf) combines independent inspection, worker complaints and remediation with escalation for noncompliance. Paragraph 22 calls for commercially feasible remediation financing; paragraph 23 includes qualified sourcing commitments. These are contractual provisions, not proof of implementation or causal effects. Paragraphs 13-15 address income during renovation, reemployment efforts and refusal of unsafe work. The published worker studies are needed to evaluate outcomes.
